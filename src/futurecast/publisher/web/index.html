The "Not a Finance-Bro" Cheat Sheet Plain-English explanations of every number on this page

Momentum

Move Last move and z
The return over the last window (1h, 4h, 1d or 3d), divided by the size of a typical move that long: z. A typical move comes from the last 14 days' volatility, so z 2 means twice the usual move, however wild the coin is. The percentile ranks it against every past move of that length.
Next After similar moves
All past moves are split into fifths, from the hardest falls to the hardest rises. For the fifth the latest move belongs to: what the next window did on average, and how often it rose. Compared with every window, which shows the coin's plain drift.
95% Range of the mean
Where the true average plausibly lies. Windows overlap (a 4h window starting every 15 minutes), so the range is widened for that (Newey–West). If it straddles zero, the average is indistinguishable from luck.
Signal Long / Short
Only when the latest move is outside the middle fifth, the average after similar moves beats a market round trip in fees (taker in and out, CoinDCX USDT-M futures with GST), its 95% range excludes zero, it shows in both the older and newer half of the history, and at least 30 non-overlapping windows stand behind it. A continuation follows the move; a reversal fades it.

Does momentum persist?

VR Variance ratio
The variance of moves of the horizon's length over what a random walk of one-candle moves would give (Lo–MacKinlay). Above 1: moves keep their direction (trending). Below 1: they reverse (mean-reverting). The p-value says how surprising the ratio would be if price were a random walk; under 0.05 counts.
IC Past → next correlation
Correlation between each move and the move of the same length that followed it, over the whole history. Positive means momentum, negative means reversal. Values of a few hundredths are normal for markets and can still matter.
Halves Older / newer
The same average computed on the older and the newer half of the history separately. An effect that shows up in only one half is more likely a fluke of that stretch.

Signals

Flow Taker flow
Share of traded value bought with market orders. Above 50%, buyers were the aggressive side; the percentile compares it with past windows.
RSI Relative strength index
Average gains against average losses over 14 candles, 0–100. Above 70 price has run up hard, below 30 down hard.
EMA Moving-average stack
Price against its 20, 50 and 200-hour exponential moving averages. An up stack (price above all three, faster above slower) is an established uptrend.
Fund Funding rate
What longs pay shorts (or the reverse when negative) at each settlement. A high percentile means crowded longs.
Book Order book imbalance
Buy versus sell limit orders waiting in the book. Heavy bids can cushion dips; heavy asks can cap rallies. Display only.
